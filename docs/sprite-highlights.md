# Sprite highlights

[#111](https://github.com/odogono/fman/issues/111). In Modern mode, matches decided as [moments](moment-highlights.md) are shown on a top-down pitch with tiny sprites, in place of the paper-and-ink ticker. The direction and mockups are in [highlights-engine.md](highlights-engine.md). The clips play with sprites ([#144](https://github.com/odogono/fman/issues/144), [#146](https://github.com/odogono/fman/issues/146)), with the moments drawer and panel ([#145](https://github.com/odogono/fman/issues/145)).

## Scope

- **A view only.** It adds no rule and no Enhanced version. It shows in Modern mode for every game decided as moments: `enhanced-7` onwards. Original-rules games keep the original pitch ([#45](https://github.com/odogono/fman/issues/45)).
- **Classic mode keeps the ticker.** A Spectrum-styled sprite view isn't planned.
- **Kits stay as they are.** Since `enhanced-7` no match is drawn on the attribute pitch, so yellow and green kits would be possible, but `enhanced-3` to `enhanced-6` still use that pitch with the same kit list. Lifting the ban would need a new kit list and Enhanced version, and is left until someone wants it. Each goalkeeper wears the first colour from a fixed list (orange, lime, grey, purple) that looks unlike both kits and the other goalkeeper, as keepers do in real football.
- **Injury clips** play an injury under [match injuries](match-injuries.md) ([#110](https://github.com/odogono/fman/issues/110)); see **Injuries** below.

## Clips

Each moment plays as a **clip** ([glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)): the build-up, the moment, then the reaction, all at real-world pace. The camera works like the 16-bit football games': it shows only a section of the pitch, follows the ball, and zooms in slightly for the moment.

- **Camera.** The pitch runs up and down the screen on every screen. The camera shows a patch the same width on every screen, about 40 m across, scaled to fit, so players are the same size everywhere; for the moment it tightens to about 30 m. The patch is 1.25 times as tall as it is wide, and grows taller where the screen leaves a taller box for the pitch, up to twice its width, so a tall phone sees more of the pitch's length, never a closer picture. On a phone on its side the patch may go as short as square, following the ball so it stays in the picture. Near either end it shows no more than 8 m beyond the goal line, as before. It looks a little ahead of the ball in the direction of play, so the goal comes into view before the shot.
- **Mini-map.** A small overview of the whole pitch in a corner shows both sides and the ball, hidden during the zoom.

| Moment    | Build-up                                | Moment                                                                                           | Reaction                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| --------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Goal      | Two to four seeded passes, with carries | The shot, and the ball in the net                                                                | The scorer celebrates and wheels away, team-mates piling in; the defenders nearest their goal hold their heads and all trudge back; the keeper fetches the ball from the net, jogging back for it from off their line |
| Save      | The same                                | The shot; the keeper dives, holds or parries, or comes to catch or punch a cross                 | The shooter holds their head, then jogs back with the attackers; the keeper walks out and throws, rolls or kicks the ball out, or after a parry gets back to their line for the corner                                |
| Clearance | The same                                | A defender cuts out the final pass, heads a cross clear, blocks the shot, or tackles the shooter | The defender clears long upfield or into touch; the attackers jog back                                                                                                                                                |
| Near miss | The same                                | The shot hits the post, goes wide or over                                                        | The shooter holds their head, then jogs back with the attackers                                                                                                                                                       |
| Injury    | The same                                | The named defender tackles the injured player, or they pull up                                   | The player stays down; the ball goes into touch, and two team-mates walk over                                                                                                                                         |

Only the build-up varies: the number of passes, their distances and the carries between them, and how the chance is finished. It starts at a seeded spot in the attacking side's own half and works up the pitch, the last pass reaching the named player in shooting range. The back line holds one depth and moves as one, letting its players get no more than 2 m apart, so it waits for anyone left behind; the defender nearest the ball steps out to close it down, and a defender out of position sprints back. Goal side of the ball and within 4 m of it, a defender jockeys: they go no more than 1 m/s faster than the ball, so they never sprint in and stop dead. No two players come within 1.3 m of each other, about a sprite's width: anyone closer steps apart. Defenders drift towards the ball and never intercept in the build-up; they tackle only in a challenge (below): a pass along the ground goes around them, and they keep a metre clear of it. Midfielders push on about 8 m ahead of the ball in support, except for a ball through. The pass before the final one goes forward, to whoever is furthest up ahead of the ball, other than the shooter, preferring an open lane, so the back line drops off and the shooter, waiting onside, can be found in range. With only the final pass left but the ball still too far out for that, the holder plays one more pass forward first, while there is time, then carries it on until the line has dropped near enough. Other passes go forward to someone in an open lane where they can; a pass back goes no more than about 11 m, to the passer's own line or the one behind. Attackers keep onside: until a pass is played to them they wait no further up than the second-last defender, the ball or halfway, and one caught beyond drops straight back. Of the build-ups, 40% start in open play, 20% each from a free kick and a throw-in, and 10% each from a goal kick and a corner, named on the match-time card. A defender or keeper who shoots has come up for a set piece: their build-up starts from a corner 60% of the time and a free kick 40%. A free kick is set between the attacking side's own half and about 35 m out, with the defenders 9.15 m back. A throw-in is held over the thrower's head beyond the touchline, then thrown to a team-mate 6 to 20 m away. A goal kick is taken by the keeper from the six-yard box and goes long to someone in midfield; a side whose only keeper is the shooter takes none. A corner is crossed from the flag to the shooter, who heads it, with up to four team-mates attacking the box; a corner to be headed takes 3 to 4 seconds to set, as the box fills. The seed also picks, evenly, a save's dive, hold or parry and a [clearance](clearances.md)'s interception or block. A near miss goes wide 60% of the time (split by side), over 25% and off the post 15%, since the original's misses rarely hit the post.

How a goal, save or near miss is **finished** comes from a stream of the seed's own, so an ordinary shot plays as it did before finishes varied. A clearance is always a shot cut out, blocked or tackled.

| Finish     | Forward | Midfielder | How it plays                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------- | ------- | ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Shot       | 50%     | 45%        | From 11 to 22 m out, after a touch. Found further out, the shooter carries it into range at 7 m/s first, but no more than 7 m from where they took the final pass: found a little further out, they shoot from up to 25 m, and further still, or too late and deep, by 13.5 seconds, they carry it only to 28 m and shoot from range. A blocked shot stays a shot, carried only as far as there is time, and no more than the same 7 m. |
| Header     | 25%     | 15%        | The ball goes wide, carried out to the wing if it is within 25 m or played out there, its receiver taking at least 25 frames over their first touch; the shooter waits in the box and never receives in the build-up. The cross is part of the moment: the shooter runs onto it, crouches, leaps and meets it at about 2 m, 6 to 11 m out, and the header goes on to goal at 14 m/s.                                                    |
| Long range | 5%      | 30%        | The shooter waits deep, 24 to 30 m out along their own line from goal, comes onto the ball, and strikes it at 28 m/s.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| One-on-one | 20%     | 10%        | The defence plays a high line; a ball goes through behind its deepest defender, who then chases from behind. The shooter runs on to about 16 m out and the keeper comes 6 m off their line. Saved, the keeper stops it where they stand; scored, they are left there. With the line too deep to get behind, it is a shot.                                                                                                               |

**Crosses that aren't headed** ([#224](https://github.com/odogono/fman/issues/224)). A ball in from wide needn't be headed at goal. About a third of each of these comes from one, picked from a stream of its own (`cross`) with one roll per clip, so every other clip plays as before; the clip says which (`cross`), and the finish mix is unchanged:

| Cross        | Of                                                | How it plays                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Low          | Shots, half the time                              | The ball goes wide as for a header, and is driven in low, at 20 m/s (slower, to 12, for a shooter further off), to the shooter 7 to 13 m out, who waits 4 m short of it, runs onto it and strikes it first time.                               |
| Cut-back     | Shots, the other half                             | A winger carries it down the line to the byline, within the depth of the six-yard box, and pulls it back low to the shooter 10 to 16 m out, central, who meets it first time. With the byline beyond a 35 m run, the ball goes in low instead. |
| Headed clear | Clearances (two in three cut-out final balls)     | The cross comes in towards the shooter 6 to 11 m out, and the defender nearest where it drops rises a stride in front of them and heads it 14 to 22 m back out. That is the clearance: no one strikes it again.                                |
| Half-cleared | Shots from range                                  | The cross comes into the box, a defender heads it out, and it loops to the shooter waiting at the edge of the box, 19 to 25 m out, who runs onto it and strikes it first time.                                                                 |
| Punched      | Saves from a header, but claims, corners included | The keeper comes for it as for a claim, but punches it 18 m back out instead of catching it, then walks back to their line.                                                                                                                    |

A keeper coming for a cross, to claim or punch it, meets it at least 2 m short of the header, who pulls up a stride short of them. A forced outcome on the debug page is never turned into a punch.

**Rebounds.** About one shot or header in five that reaches the keeper's goal is a rebound, picked from a stream of its own so other clips play as before. It needs the chance from within 20 m, and never comes from a clearance or a keeper's catch of the cross. The keeper dives to the first effort but pushes it back out, 5 to 8 m into the box. The shooter, once a header has landed, follows in at 7 m/s, eyes on goal, and strikes it again, while the nearest defender chases but stays 2.5 m short. The keeper lies down a moment, then scrambles back across their goal. The second effort ends as the moment says: scored into the side the keeper has left, saved, or missed. The clip debugger's Rebound control forces one, or none.

**Tackles.** Half the blocks of a shooter on the ball, for a shot or a shot from range, are tackles instead, picked from a stream of their own so other clips play as before: about a quarter of clearances. The defender nearest the shooter sprints in at 8 m/s and, from 3 m, slides in feet first, homing on the ball, as the shooter plants their feet to shoot. The slide shows for at least 6 frames before it meets the ball, which runs loose 3 to 6 m on across the shooter's path. The shooter stumbles on for a fifth of a second, goes down, and lies there for nearly a second before getting up. The tackler sits up, then gets up too. A team-mate within 9 m runs onto the loose ball and clears it; with none, the tackler does. Of the other blocks, half are made sliding in, over the last of the shot's flight.

**Injuries** ([match-injuries.md](match-injuries.md)). An injury plays as a tackle, by the opposing player the moment names, or by the nearest defender without club squads. Where the player pulled up instead, nobody slides: they stop short, 20 to 32 frames into their run or before a defender closing them down is 3.5 m away, and the ball runs on 3 to 5 m. It all happens outside the penalty area: the player is found, runs and takes their touch no nearer than 19.5 m from goal, so the tackle never looks like a foul that should have been a penalty. The exception is a **foul** in the box that gives a penalty ([penalties.md](penalties.md)): the player is found 11 to 14 m out, or carries the ball into the box at 7 m/s, with the tackler running alongside, a little ahead, until they are in it; the slide comes in the box, and the player goes down there, no nearer goal than 8 m. Play stops for the foul: the loose ball lies where it ran, and nobody clears it. Either way they go down and stay down to the end of the clip, where they fell, the camera on them. A defender takes the loose ball and puts it into touch, and the two nearest team-mates walk over. The name label shows over them as for any moment; there is no announcement. A player injured in a clip is left out of that match's later clips and the full-time still, so their side plays on a player short on screen, though the match was decided with them.

**Challenges.** In about a quarter of clips, a defender slides in on the ball during the build-up and the carrier skips past, picked from a stream of its own (80% of clips want one; it comes when it can). It comes during a carry at 4 m/s or more, from a non-keeper defender 2 to 5 m from the ball and in front of it, only while the carry runs on long enough for the defender to be up again before the zoom, and no later than 2 seconds before the build-up's time is up. The slide is aimed just behind where the ball will be, so the ball is knocked on past it; it runs a metre further and slows over 24 frames, then the defender sits up for 20 and gets up. The carrier skips aside, away from where the slide ends, by about 0.65 m over 20 frames, the ball staying ahead of them. The defender stays where the slide left them until they are up, and no one presses in the meantime but the next nearest.

**Penalties** ([penalties.md](penalties.md)). A goal, save or near miss from the penalty spot has no build-up and no passes. It opens on the ball on the spot, 11 m out, the card reading "Penalty", with the taker 2.6 to 3.4 m back, the keeper on their line, and everyone else outside the box and the D: along the edge of the box either side, then behind the D, then a row further back, the two sides taking turns. Everyone waits, still, for 80 to 100 frames, while the camera closes in; over the zoom's last frames the taker steps up at 4.5 m/s, and strikes it. The shot ends as the moment says, as any shot does: in, saved (held, a dive or a parry, never a rebound) or missed. A defender or keeper may take one. The set-up comes from a stream of its own, so every other clip plays as before.

**Getting up.** A keeper who catches the ball diving stays down with it until the reaction. A player down on the ground (a keeper after a dive, a tackled shooter) gets up over their last frames there: on hands and knees, then on one knee, up to 12 frames in all. From sitting up after a slide, only the knee shows.

Every corner is headed, and so is every chance a defender or keeper has, from a corner or a free kick. A side with no one but the shooter can't cross, so shoots. A carrier slows to turn with the ball and curves round rather than turning on the spot.

**Keepers.** A keeper stands on the angle, on the line from the middle of their goal to the ball, from 1.5 m off their line with the ball close to 8 m with it far away. Of the saves from a cross, about a third (picked from the finish's stream, so other saves are as before) are a **claim**, and a third of the rest a **punch** (above): the keeper sets off as the cross is struck and catches it in the air a few metres short of the header, and the clip reaches its moment in their hands. After a catch the keeper holds the ball, then throws it (45%) or rolls it (35%) to a team-mate 12 to 25 m away who stands to take it, or kicks it long (20%, and always with no one in reach). After a catch the keeper walks a few steps out with the ball as the team-mate they look for breaks wide and the rest push up. After a parry, out for a corner, the keeper gets up and back to their line, the attacker nearest the flag goes to take it, and the other attackers stay up.

**Celebrations.** After a goal the scorer throws their arms up where they scored, then wheels away at a sprint with their arms still up, towards the corner flag, to the fans behind the goal or back to their team-mates, as the seed picks. They go no more than 12 m, and only as far as leaves them time to celebrate when they get there. There, as the seed picks, they stand with their arms up, jump for joy again and again, crouching then leaping with a fist punched up, or, after a run to the corner or the fans, go down in a knee slide over the last 3 m and stay on their knees, arms spread. The three team-mates nearest make for the same spot and jump for joy around them, each in their own time, turned to the scorer; the rest jog over to a few metres off. Through a goal's reaction the camera follows the scorer, keeping them in shot rather than the ball in the net.

**The ball.** A ball in the air bends a little, from a stream of its own: a cross by 0.8 to 2 m at its widest, a lofted pass by up to 1 m and a shot by up to 1 m; a header and anything along the ground run straight. A lofted pass, a goal kick, a keeper's long kick and a clearance land at four-fifths of the way and bounce once, a fifth as high; a clearance then runs on 4 m along the grass and stops. A first touch takes the pace off the ball: it runs on the way the pass came at no more than 6 m/s faster than the receiver, slowing, and the receiver comes onto it, at least 25 frames at their own pace of 1.5 to 3.5 m/s. The ball never stops dead as it arrives, nor runs back to them.

Everything moves at real speeds: passes at about 15 m/s, runs at 5 to 7 m/s, half a second to a second to control each pass, and seeded carries of 1 to 2.5 seconds. A pass keeps its pace and the receiver is played in where they can get to it, so the ball never slows to wait for them; the final pass is held back up to 2 seconds while the shooter gets within reach (3 if they are still coming up from behind the ball), so long as it is due by 10 seconds, and never past 11; a ball through, which goes before the defence recovers, is held back up to a second, and only within its build-up's time; and up to a second while the shooter gets back onside; and a crosser waits likewise for the header to run into the box. Waiting, the holder never stands on the ball: they carry it on at 2.5 m/s, the way they were going, upfield and away from the nearest defender, picked once as the wait starts. A pass over 30 m goes in the air, and the holder carries the ball on, for up to 2 seconds, to play the final pass from within about 30 m of the shooter; before the final pass there is no other carry. Past 8 seconds the final pass is played without a carry first, in the air if it is long, though it may still be held back for the shooter as above; a cross or a ball through, which take time of their own after, are played sooner, from 6 and 5 seconds, and a run through on goal lasts at most 4 seconds. A shooter found late takes the ball in half a second, and the zoom then takes half a second. A player who passes, throws, shoots or clears faces where the ball goes. Otherwise a player faces the way they run once faster than 2.5 m/s, judged over the last 10 frames, and faces the ball again once slower than 1.5 m/s, so a player shuffling about their place watches the ball. A turn waits at least 0.4 seconds after the last, but a kick or a throw faces its aim at once. The defender closing the ball down keeps on until another is 1.5 m nearer it. A carrier keeps the ball a stride ahead and runs the way it goes. They pick up speed at about 6 m/s each second and slow at 10, easing up before where they are going; the ball comes round them at up to 4 m/s, easing in and out of the turn, and a receiver bends their run onto a pass out of the stride they are in. A build-up takes about 4 to 14 seconds, as little as 3.5 for a quick cross, so the moment starts within 15; then the zoom takes about 1, the moment until the ball arrives and 0.8 after, and the reaction 3 to 4, so clips run about 8 to 23 seconds and a match of 6 to 10 moments about 2 to 4 minutes, near the original's pace. A clip **reaches its moment** on the frame the ball crosses the line, reaches the keeper, or passes the post or byline.

- **Seed.** Everything made up in a clip comes from a seed of its own, a hash of the match and the moment: the clubs, the moment's place in the list, its minute, its kind and the named player. It never draws the game's random numbers or reads the clock, so a resumed match, or the same match shown again, plays the same clip.
- **Length.** The view sets each clip's length and the sim holds it ([ADR 0013](adr/0013-clips-set-their-own-length.md)). At kick-off, before the first frame plays, the view reads every moment from the ticker and hands the sim one length per moment, in frames (`holdClips`). Each length replaces the 75-frame pause that shows its moment; the 0-0 header and the final hold keep 75 frames. The journal saves them (save version 12). Classic mode hands over nothing.
- **Decided at kick-off.** A match plays as clips only if the view handed over clip lengths at its kick-off, and then for the whole match, resumes included. A match without them, such as one saved before clips existed or kicked off under reduced motion, shows the ticker to the end.
- **Resuming mid-clip.** The sim's ticker says how many frames into the current pause the match is, so a resumed clip picks up at that frame.
- **Between clips** a wipe sweeps across: thin diagonal stripes, each 10 art pixels wide, in the home kit, white and the away kit, edged in ink, travel from left to right with the next picture behind them. It takes 0.8 s, played over the last 0.4 s of one picture and the first 0.4 s of the next, so the clips keep their lengths; each picture holds still at its end while the other plays. It runs from the kick-off still into the first clip, between clips, and from the last clip into the full-time still. Ended early by a tap, a keypress or Skip, the picture left wipes away over the first 0.8 s of the next; ended mid-wipe, the view cuts straight to the newest picture. Each clip opens with both sides already in shape, under the match-time card, which is part of the clip's own length; there is no filler play.
- **The match-time card.** A clip opens with a card in the top left: ink with a white edge, a stripe in the kit of the side with the ball, the minute in large yellow figures and, under it, the restart: FREE KICK, CORNER, THROW-IN or GOAL KICK. From open play it shows the minute alone. It slides in from the left, holds and slides out over the clip's first 1.5 s, or holds until a restart is taken if that is longer.
- **The goal announcement.** When a goal goes in, GOAL! drops in letter by letter in the middle of the picture, big white letters with an ink outline and a drop shadow, the ! in yellow, while confetti in the scorer's kit falls. Under the letters a tile shows the new score, home first, in ink on white, beside the scorer's name and the minute on their kit colour (in ink on a light kit). It shows from the moment for 2.5 s, or less if the clip ends sooner, and is gone, its letters falling away, before the wipe out of the clip starts. The scorer's name label is hidden while it shows. A name too long for the tile drops to the small letters.
- **Kick-off and full time.** During the 0-0 header both sides stand in shape with the ball on the centre spot, under a card reading KICK-OFF. The final hold shows the same still pitch under FULL TIME, or the full ticker list after Skip.
- **Ends.** The sides change ends at half-time: the home side attacks up the screen before 46′ and down after.
- **Players.** The managed club shows one sprite per picked player, set out by position: defence at the back, midfield in the middle, attack up front. A picked goalkeeper keeps goal; with none, the first-picked defender does, so even a side picked short has a keeper. A player with no position plays in midfield. A goalkeeper the moment names as its shooter plays up front, and the same rule picks who keeps goal instead. A team picked short of 11 plays short. Each line spreads evenly across the pitch at a fixed depth, whatever its count. The opponent fields a 4-4-2 in squad order: its first goalkeeper, then its first four defenders, four midfielders and two attackers, filling any gap from other positions. The named player always plays, in place of the last-listed player in their position. Only the player the moment names is labelled, in the zoomed view, except while a goal is announced.
- **Tiredness.** In the zoomed view, a tired player shows a tired [status icon](#status-icons). A managed player's chance of showing it is `(1 − energy/20) × minute/90` from their own energy; an opponent's uses the side's team Energy, since other clubs' players' energy isn't kept. At most three players a side show it, the named player and keepers included. It is decided once per clip, from the clip's seed only.

## The screen

- **The pitch takes the screen.** On a phone the screen is three parts: the scoreboard strip, the pitch filling everything between, and one row of controls under it. The top bar, its title and the page's footer are hidden, and the page keeps clear of the notch and the home bar.
- **On its side.** Where the window is wider than tall and no more than 500 px tall, the pitch stands at full height in the middle, still running up the screen, between two columns of equal width. The left holds the scoreboard stacked: the game menu button, the home club, the score, the away club and the clock, with full names wrapping as needed. The right holds the moments panel, newest first, reading Kick-off before the first, with Skip all, then Continue, at its foot. There is no drawer and no line under the pitch, and after Skip the pitch shows the still Full time picture.
- **Scoreboard.** One strip: the game menu button, the home club, the score, the away club, each club in its name colour, and the clock. The competition line is left out, since it showed when the match was picked. Where a club's name is longer than 10 letters on a phone, both clubs show their first three letters in capitals, unless the two would read the same, when the full names stay and are cut short. The clock shows the minute of the clip on show from the clip's start, 0′ before the first and FT after the last. The score, and the list of moments, change when a clip reaches its moment, never when it starts, so nothing gives the moment away.
- **Controls.** The row under the pitch holds the latest moment's line and Skip all, in yellow. After the last clip, for the final hold, Skip all becomes Continue, which ends the hold as a keypress would.
- **Moments drawer (phone).** In the row under the pitch, the latest moment's ticker line is a button ending in ☰ and the count of lines shown, and screen readers hear it as "All moments" with the count. It opens a drawer: a sheet rising from the bottom of the pitch, at most about half its height, so the clip plays on above it. The drawer lists every line shown so far, newest first, the latest included, and scrolls; it closes from its own Close button, or a second tap on the line. Until the first clip reaches its moment the line reads Kick-off, and after Skip, Full time, with no button.
- **Moments panel (wide window).** Where Modern mode shows two panes, the panel takes the left column, 360px wide, in the space the hidden Home pane leaves, with the match in the right column. It lists the same lines, newest first, and replaces the line and drawer under the pitch, so Skip all stands alone in the row under it.
- **Both.** Neither pauses the clip: lines arrive as each clip reaches its moment, with the drawer open or not. Whether the drawer is open is the view's alone: not saved, closed at each match's start, on resuming and when the highlights end. The button reports it as `aria-expanded`. Enter or Space on the button opens the drawer and never reaches the sim, so it doesn't end the clip. A tap on the pitch, even with the drawer open, ends the clip as before and leaves the drawer as it is. Screen readers hear each line once, when its clip reaches the moment; the drawer and panel are plain lists that announce nothing.
- **Keys.** A tap or keypress during a clip ends it and moves to the next moment, as a keypress ends a ticker pause. Cut short before its moment, the clip still shows the moment's score, ticker line and screen-reader line before the cut. Skip all ends every remaining clip. On a phone it closes the drawer, removes its button and shows the full ticker list over the pitch for the final hold; on a wide window the panel already holds the list, so the pitch shows the still "Full time" picture. The result screen follows as now.
- **Reduced motion.** When the browser asks for less motion, the view hands the sim no clip lengths and shows the paper-and-ink ticker instead.
- **Screen readers** hear each moment's ticker line when its clip reaches the moment.

## Artwork

New sprites drawn for this view, larger than the original's 8×8 characters: about 14 by 22 art pixels, with a big head and a face, in a three-quarter view as in the 16-bit football games. Each faces one of four directions, with up, down and left drawn and right mirrored. The clip records each player's facing, from their movement or towards the ball. The poses are stand, run (a cycle of four frames, two strides, with arms swinging and a bounce on each passing step), kick (for both passing and shooting), header (a crouch, a leap with the feet off the ground, and the forehead meeting the ball, then the crouch again as they land), dive, hold (the ball held up, by a keeper or a thrower), head (hands on head after a near miss), celebrate (arms up), a celebrating run with arms up (four frames, as the run), a jump for joy (a crouch, then in the air with a fist up), a knee slide, a sliding tackle (sliding, then sitting up), a fall (tripped, then lying face down) and getting up (on hands and knees, then on one knee). The dive, the tackle, the fall and getting up are drawn side on, facing left or right. A frame in the air keeps clear rows under its feet. The ball has a few spin frames and its shadow.

- **Drawn on a canvas** of its own at the screen's resolution. Each art pixel covers a whole number of device pixels, rounded from the camera's scale, and sprites sit on whole device pixels, so the art stays crisp. Through the zoom a sprite steps up one size rather than growing smoothly.
- **Tinted when drawn.** The shirt takes the kit colour, the shorts and socks a fixed darker shade of it, with a dark outline. A keeper's shirt takes the goalkeeper colour. Skin and hair come from small palettes, picked by a hash of the player's name, so a player looks the same in every clip and match. Nothing about looks is stored.
- **Lettering.** The cards, the goal announcement and the named player's label use the highlights' own pixel font, 5 by 7 art pixels, with accented letters drawn plain, on a grid of art pixels that scales with the picture: it is 210 art pixels across, each a whole number of device pixels, so the lettering takes the same share of the picture on every screen and stays crisp.
- **Pitch.** The markings stay drawn as lines, over mown stripes. The mini-map keeps its dots; the kick-off and full-time stills use the stand pose. The ball is 7 art pixels across.
- **Source.** The sprites are pixel grids written as text in `web/src/view/assets`. They began as reference images made for this project with ChatGPT's image tool, which were converted to grids in the sprites' palette and cleaned up by hand; only the grids are kept, with their provenance in the assets README. The original's characters stay with the original pitch, and the mockups in [highlights-engine.md](highlights-engine.md) are for direction only.

The clip engine shipped first with placeholder shapes, a kit-coloured circle per player and a white dot for the ball ([#144](https://github.com/odogono/fman/issues/144)); the sprites replaced them in [#146](https://github.com/odogono/fman/issues/146). The named player's label is spelled as on the ticker line, in capitals, above their head and any status icon. A held ball is drawn in the holder's hands, not on its own. A runner's frame changes with each 0.8 m they cover, counted along their path whichever way they run, and the ball's spin with the ground it rolls, so both keep pace with the speed. The ball is drawn 2.5 m up for each unit of its height, so a header's ball meets the leaping forehead.

### Goals and stadium

The ground is drawn in the same art at the same scale, 11 art pixels to the metre ([#157](https://github.com/odogono/fman/issues/157)). The stands' concrete is plain grey, with a step for each row of fans. The camera keeps up to 8 m beyond the lines in view, so only the edge of the stadium shows.

- **Goals.** Posts, crossbar and a dotted net, light and sparse on the roof and grey and dense on the back, drawn twice: the top goal seen from the front, its net rising behind the crossbar, and the bottom goal seen from behind, its net in front. Players and the ball stand in front of or behind a goal by their depth on the screen.
- **Shadows.** Pixel art, dithered in a checkerboard, lit from the top left: each goal casts its footprint down and to the right by its height, and each player and the ball cast a small shadow at their feet.
- **Around the pitch.** A strip of grass, then pitch-side advertising boards about 3 m beyond the touchlines and goal lines, then stands with a crowd. The boards are plain colour panels with stripes and shapes, without text or brands. Nothing goes through them: a ball along the ground, such as a shot wide or a clearance into touch, runs into the boards and comes back off them a little, while one higher than the boards, such as a shot over the bar, clears them and lands in the crowd. Players stop in front of them.
- **Crowd.** A repeating block of fans, mostly in the home side's kit. In a goal's reaction it stands and raises its arms; at all other times it sits. The cheer follows the clip's phase and outcome, so it draws nothing from the seed.
- **Source.** Generated references set the look. The goals and boards are drawn to scale as pixel grids by script: a goal mouth is 81 art pixels, 7.32 m. The fans are the players' own stand and celebrate poses cut at the waist, in each stand's facing: towards the camera behind the top goal, backs turned behind the bottom one, and side-on along the touchlines. Each seat's shirt, skin and hair come from a hash of its place.

### Status icons

A **status icon** is a small pixel-art icon floating above a player's head, after the thought bubbles of _The Sims_, at the sprites' scale. It shows only in the zoomed view: in the wide view it would be too small to read. A player shows at most one; when more than one applies, a fixed order picks it. The sprite artwork brings only the tired icon: a blue sweat drop in a white thought bubble. Later states, such as injuries during the match ([#110](https://github.com/odogono/fman/issues/110)), take the same place.

## Debug page

A page of its own, `web/clips.html`, served by `bun run dev` at `/clips` and published beside the game at `clips.html` (left out of the offline cache), plays any clip the engine can make. It sets the kind, outcome, restart and finish, whether an injury was a tackle and whether that tackle was a foul in the box, whether a goal, save or near miss is a penalty (or leaves them to the seed), a cross that isn't headed at goal or none (or leaves it to the seed), the scoring side, the minute, both kits, the managed side's line counts with or without a goalkeeper, and the moment's place in the list, with a random-seed button. It plays, pauses, steps and scrubs frames at ¼×, 1× and 4×, shows the clip's length, moment frame and passes, and can overlay player ids and the ball's path. It sets each side's energy, to show tiredness. It can also load the autosave or a web save file and replay a moment from that match's ticker. In the browser's console, `clipDebug` gives the clip on show and its frame. It also has `paint(frame)`, which draws a frame at once, even in a background tab, and `set(settings)`, which changes the controls and makes the clip again. `bun run clips:record` uses both to record clips as videos ([tools/clips](../tools/clips/README.md)).

It also checks each clip for play that looks wrong (`web/src/view/clip-checks.ts`):

- passes that face away, crawl, run through a defender, go long along the ground or a long way back
- receivers who miss the ball or turn sharply
- a ball that trails its carrier, or jumps at their feet: its speed changing by more than 6 m/s in a frame off a pass's flight
- a holder standing still on the ball in the build-up: within 1.5 m of it, moving slower than 0.5 m/s, for more than 0.4 s, after the first pass
- players standing inside each other
- a scattered back line
- a player turning three times in half a second, but to kick
- a moment that starts more than 15 seconds in
- a shot struck more than 32 m out
- a long run to shoot: the shooter carrying the ball more than 12 m between the final pass and the zoom, but for a one-on-one
- a defender or keeper scoring from open play or a throw-in

The clip's problems are listed under its readout, each jumping to its first frame. The Problems overlay rings the players involved and names each problem while it shows. "Check 200 seeds" counts the clips each problem shows in, over 200 seeds with the other settings as they are, or over every moment of a loaded match, and opens any of the first few with one. The clip tests hold the engine to the same checks.

## Order of work

1. **Clip lengths from the view** (sim, done in [#143](https://github.com/odogono/fman/issues/143)): the ticker's timeline takes a length for each moment, saves it, and defaults to 75 frames.
2. **The sprite view with placeholder shapes** (Modern, done in [#144](https://github.com/odogono/fman/issues/144)): the clip scripts, seeding, camera and zoom, mini-map, ends, team shapes, the goalkeeper colour, cuts, Skip, resuming mid-clip, reduced motion and screen readers. Only the latest moment's ticker line shows under the pitch. The debug page ships with it.
3. **The moments drawer and panel** (Modern, done in [#145](https://github.com/odogono/fman/issues/145)).
4. **The sprite artwork** (done in [#146](https://github.com/odogono/fman/issues/146)): the sprites, facing, the new poses and the tired status icon.
5. **Goals and stadium** (done in [#157](https://github.com/odogono/fman/issues/157)): the goals, boards and crowd, and a larger ball.
